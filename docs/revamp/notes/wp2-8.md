### Behaviour

- **Accept and pre-auth bounds (invariant 22).**
  - The `net-accept` thread accepts connections. It waits on a `polling` readiness poller, which `shutdown` (or the daemon's `ctx.shutdown`) wakes (Deviation 2).
  - Some accept errors concern only the one connection: reset or aborted before it was taken (`WSAECONNRESET`, `ECONNABORTED`), and the network errors Linux's `accept(2)` says to retry on. These are skipped, and the next connection is taken at once. Other errors (out of descriptors or memory, unknown) are logged, and the loop pauses 100 ms instead of spinning. Before review finding 1 every error paused, so a client sending a stream of reset connections could delay every legitimate dial by 100 ms each.
  - At most 32 connections are in the pre-auth handshake (`InboundOptions::max_preauth`). Each handshake runs on its own `net-conn` thread. A connection beyond the 32 is closed at once, before anything is read from it (`refused_preauth`).
  - The handshake is WP1.10's `session::accept_v3`. Every message is ≤ 16 KiB, every step ≤ 5 s, and each step has a watchdog. Tungstenite refuses an HTTP upgrade request above 64 KiB.
  - Measured with 100 sockets each sending 1 MiB of garbage (half random bytes, half an endless HTTP token): live heap grew by 0.77–1.04 MB (three runs). The pre-auth peak stayed at 32. A legitimate dial started with the flood succeeded in 19–25 ms, on its second attempt.
- **Session cap.**
  - At most 128 sessions (`max_sessions`). `admit` reserves the session slot at `HELLO`, before `AUTH`. A dialer beyond the cap is answered `GOAWAY{TOO_MANY_SESSIONS}` in about 2 ms (debug build).
  - A handshake that was admitted and then failed gives its slot back: the per-connection policy releases it in `Drop`. The release happens when the server's handshake ends. After its `GOAWAY` the server drains for the dialer's close (≤ 1 s), so a dialer can learn of the failure a moment before the slot is free. Pre-auth slots and registrations are guards too, so even a panic gives a slot back.
- **`AcceptPolicy`** (`CatalogAcceptPolicy`) answers from the catalog snapshot, O(1):
  - `installation` gives `ctx.installation`, so a dialer with our own installation is refused (self-pair);
  - `verifier` gives the verifier and `verifier_generation`;
  - `admit` refuses a revoked installation (`peer_meta.revoked_ms`; nothing sets it before WP4.1);
  - `legacy_attempt` records `StatusEvent::InboundIncompatible{address, detail}`. The detail is `PEER_NEEDS_UPGRADE: legacy (v2) peer "<installation>" …`. The installation is truncated to 64 bytes with control characters escaped. The code was already redacted by WP1.10, and it is never recorded.
- **Sessions.**
  - Each session runs with the handler pool of its role: 4 threads for control and probe sessions, 2 for bulk, queue 16 (`ConnOptions` from `model::orchestrator::limits`). A full queue answers `BUSY` (session layer).
  - The per-connection thread registers the session, serves it, waits for it to close and unregisters it.
- **Per-request checks**, in order:
  1. the daemon is not stopping (`ORCHESTRATOR_STOPPING`);
  2. the session's verifier generation is still the catalog's (`PAIRING_CODE_CHANGED`);
  3. the peer is not revoked (`PEER_REVOKED`);
  4. `model::protocol::op_allowed(op, role)`. On failure: `objects.*` off a bulk session gives `BULK_ON_CONTROL_LANE`; `meta.exchange` off a control session, and `catalog.list` on a bulk session, give `METADATA_ON_BULK_LANE`.
- **Routing.**

  | Op | Handling |
  |---|---|
  | `ping` | the session's I/O thread answers it; a streamed `ping` gets `{alive:true}` here |
  | `catalog.list` | `PublishedCatalogApi::list()`. It never calls a worker. `unchanged:true` with no entries when `since` equals the generation |
  | `meta.exchange` | parse the header `{repo}` and the `MetaExchangeRequest` body (`INVALID_REQUEST`); header and body repos agree (`REPOSITORY_MISMATCH`); resolve the folder; `request.receiver` is the folder's device (`LOCAL_REPLICA_BINDING_MISMATCH`). The route is `{peer: authenticated installation, repo, local_replica: folder device, remote_replica: request.sender}`. Then worker `feed-exchange` (25 s, capped by the peer's deadline minus 250 ms). The reply must be bound (`reply.reply_to == request.request`, else `UNBOUND_PEER_RESPONSE`) and well formed (`INVALID_REPLY`) |
  | `objects.exchange` | `inbound_objects`: bulk role (`BULK_ON_CONTROL_LANE`); the page's lane is the session's authenticated lane (`BULK_LANE_MISMATCH`); resolve the folder. The route is `{peer: authenticated installation, repo, local_replica: device, remote_replica: page.sender}`. Then worker `published-bulk-exchange{route, lane, page}` (60 s, capped by the peer's deadline) |
  | other `objects.*` | `UNSUPPORTED_OPERATION` (WP3.3) |
  | anything else | `UNKNOWN_PEER_OPERATION` |

  Folder resolution checks the catalog record first: an unknown repo gives `REPOSITORY_NOT_MANAGED`, and `sync_enabled == false` gives `FOLDER_SYNC_PAUSED`. The FolderView must then be `Running` and have a worker client, else `REMOTE_WORKER_STARTING`. None of these refusals reaches a worker, and none is recorded as an exchange. Every refusal is a Remote error, so the session stays usable (invariant 17).
- **Exchanges never hold a handler thread** (Deviation 1). `meta.exchange` and `objects.*` run on a `net-exchange` thread of their own. So `catalog.list` and every refusal answer at once, even while all of a session's exchanges wait on a hung worker. Measured: slowest of 20 `catalog.list` calls 0.5 ms, with four `meta.exchange` calls hung on a worker. Two caps apply; beyond either the answer is `BUSY` (Contention, `retry_after_ms: 50`):
  - **per session**, as many exchanges as its role has handler threads (4 or 2);
  - **server-wide**, `InboundOptions::max_exchanges` (128).

  An exchange keeps both slots until its worker call returns, also after its session is gone. A peer that drops and redials sessions therefore cannot pile up worker calls or threads (review finding 2). A request that was cancelled, expired, or whose session closed while it was queued is dropped without a reply and without a worker call. It is checked when its handler takes it, when its exchange thread starts, and again just before the worker call.
- **Status before the reply.** An exchange records its `ExchangeFinished` (and sends its gate evidence) *before* it sends the reply. Status readers see the exchange as soon as the dialer has the reply, and the slot is free a moment after. A dialer that runs exactly its slot count back to back is therefore not refused `BUSY` because of hub bookkeeping.
- **Worker errors.**
  - A worker that cannot be reached (`CallError::Unavailable`: nothing was sent) is answered `REMOTE_WORKER_STARTING`, because to the peer the remote folder is not serving. The hub records the local code (e.g. `LOCAL_WORKER_UNAVAILABLE`).
  - Every other `CallError` reaches the peer with its own code and class (`to_wire`).
- **Rotation and revocation.** The `net-rotation` thread waits on `CatalogApi::wait_newer`, which wakes at once on a change.
  - When the verifier generation changes (or the verifier disappears), every session authenticated under another generation gets `GOAWAY{PAIRING_CODE_CHANGED}`. Measured: every session was closed well within 1 s.
  - A revoked installation's sessions get `GOAWAY{PEER_REVOKED}`.
  - A session whose handshake completes while the verifier rotates is checked when it registers. That check runs under the same lock the watcher scans with, so the session is either seen by the watcher or sees the new catalog.
  - Requests that arrive before the `GOAWAY` lands are refused by the per-request generation check.
- **Shutdown.** `shutdown(deadline)` stops accepting and sends `GOAWAY{ORCHESTRATOR_STOPPING}` to every session. It then joins the accept and rotation threads and waits, until the deadline at most, for every session, admitted handshake, pre-auth handshake and worker call to end. Finally it closes whatever sessions remain. It is idempotent.
  - A cancelled `ctx.shutdown` stops the server the same way, without the wait. Dropping the `InboundServer` does too.
  - With WP1.INT's GOAWAY semantics, an idle dialer closes as soon as it reads the `GOAWAY`. A busy dialer closes once its in-flight calls end, or the 5 s grace closes the session.
- **The rotation watcher's starting point** is the catalog generation read in `start_with`, before the accept thread can register any session. A rotation between start-up and the watcher thread's first wait is therefore still a change it sees (review note).
- **Visibility (RC12).**
  - **`InboundSession{installation, lane, role, opened}`** when a control or bulk session registers and unregisters. The status lane is 0 for control and `index + 1` for bulk lane `index`, matching the hub's `legacy_lane_role` convention (lane 0 is control).
  - **`ExchangeFinished{direction: inbound}`** for every exchange a worker served, ok or failed, with the real duration:
    - for `meta.exchange`: the worker's own `local.summary`, with `bytes_up`/`bytes_down` filled from the wire body sizes when the worker left them 0;
    - for `objects.exchange`: object bytes received and sent, and the objects the request page completed (`objects_down`; WP1.INT's rule: completed objects only). `objects_up` is 0, because only the peer knows whether it installed what we sent.
  - **`InboundIncompatible`** for a legacy hello (from `legacy_attempt`) and for `PEER_PROTOCOL_UNSUPPORTED`.
  - `ExchangeStarted` is **not** sent: the hub books it on an outbound lane and marks the pair outbound-seen (CCR-WP2.8-3).
  - A served `meta.exchange` also sends `FolderCmd::GateEvidence{installation: authenticated peer, at_ms, caught_up, blocked, waiting_on}` from the acceptor's own `local.gate` view. The controller filters by gate source, and it records which paired peers give evidence (for retargeting).
  - The test `the_serving_side_reports_status`, with a real `StatusHub`, checks the result:
    - the dialer shows as an inbound-only peer, `direction: inbound`, `inbound_sessions: 2`, with lanes 0 (control) and 1 (bulk) Ready;
    - a served exchange puts the pair in the folder's `sync.peers` (`direction: inbound`, the summary's `to_receive`);
    - closing the sessions clears them.
- **Logging.** Handshake failures are logged with `util::log` (`info`, rate limited per code) and counted (`InboundStats`). Only registered codes are used: the `contracts` code-literal scan passes.

## Deviations from the spec, and why

1. **Exchanges run off the handler pool.** §3.8 sizes the pool (4/2 threads, queue 16), and so does this package. But `meta.exchange` and `objects.*` do not run on the pool: they run on a thread per request, capped per session at the role's handler count and server-wide at `max_exchanges` (128), with `BUSY` beyond either cap.
   - With exchanges on the pool, four hung `feed-exchange` calls would hold every control handler. `catalog.list` (the dialer's 2 s catalog poll, 10 s deadline) would then queue behind them for up to 25 s. That is the RC8 coupling WP2.8's acceptance test targets ("catalog.list < 100 ms with one hung worker").
   - The per-session cap equals the dialer's own concurrency: the MetaScheduler runs ≤ 4 exchanges per peer, and the interim bulk lane runs one exchange at a time. So a well-behaved dialer never sees `BUSY` from it. The server-wide cap bounds threads and worker calls across sessions and redials. It is reached only with many busy peers, or with worker calls held by a hung worker.
   - The pool queue still bounds everything else.
2. **Accept waits on a `polling` poller instead of a plain blocking `accept`.** The behaviour is the same, but the loop can be interrupted (invariant 29): `shutdown`, or the daemon's `ctx.shutdown`, wakes it with `Poller::notify` (a cancel hook). There is no wake-up connection to the server's own address and no timeout polling. `polling` 3.11 is already a dependency (§11.2). Accept errors that concern only one connection (reset or aborted before it was taken) are skipped at once. Any other error (out of descriptors, for example) is logged and the loop pauses 100 ms, so it never spins.
3. **The session cap refuses at `HELLO` with `GOAWAY{TOO_MANY_SESSIONS}`**, rather than closing the TCP connection at accept time. A dialer learns why it was refused (`DialError::Rejected(TOO_MANY_SESSIONS)`), and the refusal still takes about 2 ms. The pre-auth cap does close at once ("extra connections are closed immediately"). Only an authenticated-looking `HELLO` reaches the session check; its slot is held for at most one 5 s step.
4. **Probe sessions are not reported as `InboundSession`.** The hub keys inbound lanes by lane number only, so a probe on lane 0 would rename the control lane's role and inflate `inbound_sessions` while it lasts. Probes are short-lived pairing and listing sessions. They count toward the session cap, receive rotation `GOAWAY`s, and appear in `InboundServer::sessions()`. See CCR-WP2.8-4.
5. **Role-violation codes where §3.8 is silent:**
   - `catalog.list` on a bulk session gives `METADATA_ON_BULK_LANE` (the catalog is control traffic);
   - `meta.exchange` on a probe session gives `METADATA_ON_BULK_LANE`. §3.8 literally says "requires role=control (else `METADATA_ON_BULK_LANE`)"; the message names the actual role;
   - `objects.*` on a probe session gives `BULK_ON_CONTROL_LANE`.
6. **Revocation is honoured now.** `peer_meta.revoked_ms` refuses the handshake (`PEER_REVOKED`, a handshake GOAWAY code) and sends live sessions away. §3.8 says "from Wave 4". Nothing sets `revoked_ms` before WP4.1, so this is inert until then. WP4.1 then only needs to set it.
7. **Checks beyond the table:**
   - per request: generation (`PAIRING_CODE_CHANGED`) and stopping (`ORCHESTRATOR_STOPPING`; the legacy acceptor did both, `peering.rs:296-300`);
   - `REPOSITORY_MISMATCH` when the `meta.exchange` header and body name different repositories;
   - `LOCAL_REPLICA_BINDING_MISMATCH` when `request.receiver` is not this folder's replica. The route's `local_replica` comes from the folder, so the worker could not tell;
   - the worker's reply must be bound (`UNBOUND_PEER_RESPONSE`) and well formed (`INVALID_REPLY`) before it reaches the peer.

   All of these codes are registered (§8 or `TRANSIENT_DEFAULT_CODES`).
8. **An unreachable worker is `REMOTE_WORKER_STARTING` to the peer** (it is Transient, and the hub reads it as "waiting for the remote"). The local status keeps the real code.
9. **`objects.exchange` replies are trimmed** to the 1 MiB body bound that `model::protocol::max_body` gives the op. This is the adapter for CCR-WP2.8-1.
10. **Additive public API** beyond `start` and `shutdown`: `start_with` and `InboundOptions` (`max_preauth`, `max_sessions`, `max_exchanges`, `conn`; tests and diagnostics), `InboundStats`/`stats()`, `InboundSessionInfo`/`sessions()`, `local_addr()`, and `CatalogAcceptPolicy`. The frozen signatures (`start`, `shutdown`, `inbound_objects::handle`) are as specified; `seam_stubs` pins the latter.

No existing test or assertion was changed.

## CCRs filed

- **CCR-WP2.8-1 (`model::protocol::max_body`, frozen; for WP2.INT).**
  - *Requested change:* `max_body(ops::OBJECTS_EXCHANGE)` should return `frame::MAX_BODY_META` (16 MiB) instead of `MAX_CHUNK` (1 MiB).
  - *Reason:* the interim path (§6.5) carries legacy `bulk_sync::Page`s. `published-bulk-*` builds them with up to 4 MiB of hex parts (`PAGE_BUDGET`), and their inventories may list 100,000 known and 100,000 wanted hashes (about 13 MB). With 1 MiB:
    - a dialer request page above 1 MiB is a `PROTOCOL_VIOLATION`, and the server's session layer closes the bulk session (`io.rs` `on_request`);
    - a reply above 1 MiB is answered `WS_MESSAGE_TOO_LARGE`.

    The objects would still move, about 0.5 MiB of object bytes per round trip, but a large inventory would wedge a lane.
  - *Adapter (acceptor side):* the reply page drops object parts, last ones first, until it fits (`inbound_objects::fit_page`). Offers are stateless, so the dropped parts are offered again in a later round. A page that does not fit even without parts is answered `BULK_PAGE_TOO_LARGE`. Request pages cannot be adapted here. Until this CCR is applied, WP2.7's dialer must keep its request pages ≤ 1 MiB, or apply the same trim to its offer.
- **CCR-WP2.8-2 (§6.2 op table; spec ratification, no type change; WP2.6, WP2.7, WP2.INT).**
  - *Requested change:* state the framing of each op, as in *Wire shapes* above. Only `meta.exchange`'s request framing is explicit today.
  - *Reason:* WP2.6 (`catalog.list` client, `remote_list`) and WP2.7 (`meta.exchange`, `objects.exchange` dialers) are written in parallel with this package. A mismatch, such as a header-only catalog parser, fails only at integration.
  - *Adapter:* readers should parse a non-empty body before the header. The overflow header deliberately makes header-only parsing fail instead of reading an empty catalog. `objects.exchange` also accepts a header-borne page when the body is empty.
- **CCR-WP2.8-3 (`model::status::StatusEvent::ExchangeStarted`, frozen; plus `hub.rs`, WP1.11's).**
  - *Requested change:* `#[serde(default)] direction: Direction` on `ExchangeStarted` (default `Outbound`, so existing producers are unchanged). The hub should then book an inbound start on the inbound lane, without `outbound_seen`.
  - *Reason:* today the hub puts every `ExchangeStarted` on an outbound lane and marks the pair outbound-seen. An acceptor-side start would make an inbound-only peer look like an outbound one.
  - *Adapter:* the inbound server sends no `ExchangeStarted`. Inbound lanes therefore never show in-flight ops; finished exchanges, rates and per-pair summaries are complete.
- **CCR-WP2.8-4 (`hub.rs`, WP1.11's; optional).**
  - *Requested change:* key inbound lanes by role as well as lane number (or count probe sessions separately).
  - *Reason:* lets the serving side report probe sessions (Deviation 4).
  - *Adapter:* probe sessions are not reported.

## Known limitations

- **Not wired.** The daemon keeps serving the network through WP2.1's legacy bridge in Wave 2. WP3.1 runs `inbound::start` on the listener and calls `shutdown`.
- **One thread per exchange.** It is bounded per session (4/2) and server-wide (`max_exchanges`, 128). So there are at most 128 exchange threads, plus one `net-conn` thread per session or handshake (≤ 128 + 32), plus the sessions' handler pools and I/O threads. A worker call whose request was cancelled runs on until the worker answers (≤ 25 s for feed, ≤ 60 s for objects) and keeps its slots meanwhile. A dialer whose calls keep timing out therefore sees `BUSY` until those return. Memory is bounded by the session layer's reassembly bounds, which WP1.INT's review recorded (about 320 MiB per control connection); the exchange cap adds no request bodies beyond those already received.
- **A failed handshake's session slot** is held until the server's handshake ends, up to the 1 s close drain after its `GOAWAY` (WP1.10's `close_gracefully`). `AcceptPolicy` has no failure hook. Near the cap, a dialer retrying at once can be refused `TOO_MANY_SESSIONS` (Transient) during that second. Likewise, a dialer that sends `HELLO` and then stalls holds a reservation for up to the 5 s `AUTH` step. Since the pre-auth cap is 32, at most 32 session slots can be held that way at once.
- **Slowloris bound.** 32 silent connections can delay legitimate dials for up to one 5 s handshake step at a time. That is the §3.8 bound, and the pre-auth cap keeps it from growing.
- **No per-installation session cap.** One authenticated peer could take all 128 sessions. A well-behaved dialer uses at most 16 lanes plus probes.
- **Interim objects path** is limited to about 0.5 MiB of object bytes per reply, and to ≤ 1 MiB request pages, until CCR-WP2.8-1.
- `objects_up` of inbound `objects.exchange` is 0 (see *Visibility*). Upload progress on the acceptor shows through content counters and rates.
- Inbound lanes show no in-flight ops (CCR-WP2.8-3), and probe sessions are not reported (CCR-WP2.8-4).
- `InboundStats` is not yet exposed through `diagnostics` (WP4.2 owns `diagnostics.rs`).
