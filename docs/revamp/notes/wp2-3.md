### Startup (task 1)

`worker::run` opens the store with `OpenOptions{defer_activation: true,
verify: Changed}`: staged rows are classified, not activated, so a large inbox
no longer delays Ready (deferred activation is not recovery: invariant 24 is
unchanged, the listener still opens after `Store::open` and `Engine::new`).
After the `ReadyLine` (§4.1 step 10) it starts the activator, maintenance and
notifier threads; a thread that cannot start is logged (`WORKER_START`), never
a `FatalLine` after Ready. The heartbeat thread still starts at step 6 (WP1.4).

### Activator (task 2, §4.4)

One loop on the `activator` thread:

1. wait for `ctx.activation` (poked by staging paths and `revalidate`) or
   `ACTIVATOR_IDLE_WAIT` (1 s, due retries), unless the last slice left more;
2. **yield** to the engine's other users, at most `MAX_YIELD` (50 ms): while a
   thread waits for or holds the engine, or the engine's hold count moved
   since the activator's own last release, it waits until nobody used the
   engine for `WAITER_GRACE` (3 ms). A WinFsp operation is ~8 short callbacks
   that rarely block: the callbacks that queued behind a slice run in the gap
   right after it, so waiters alone never showed them (measured: the first
   version never yielded once in a 2K catch-up);
3. under the engine lock (marked in `EngineVitals::activator_hold`):
   `activate_slice` with §4.4's `{64 events, 25 ms, inline_slow: false}`, or
   `BUSY_SLICE` `{16, 25 ms}` while other users were active in the last
   250 ms (deviation 3); then `Engine::after_activation`;
4. a `SlowTicket` is prepared off-lock (`Store::prepare_slow` on a pooled
   `SyncStore`) and committed under the lock (`commit_prepared`; with WP1.6's
   `Prepared::Inline` the slow path still runs there until WP2.4 merges, which
   changes nothing here). `Stale` → `requeue(id, 250 ms → 30 s)` per event,
   never inline; `activation.starved_since_ms` is set while tickets keep going
   stale and cleared by the next commit;
5. counters (`ActivationCounters`: activated, fast, slow, quarantined, retried,
   stale, slices, last slice ms, last activation, starvation), then
   `status_generation += 1` and a cheap cell refresh (which wakes the
   heartbeat).

It sets `ctx.activator_running` and the engine's `background_activation`.
Errors back off (`Backoff::inbox`, 1 s → 5 min) and are logged; a panic is
contained, and a poisoned engine follows the poison policy. A slice that left
more rows but committed nothing pauses 5 ms (no spinning on classification or
deferrals).

### `after_activation` and notifications (task 3, task 7)

`Engine::activation_delta(ids, before)` reads the committed payloads (point
lookups, a light `changes[{entity, field}]` parse) into
`ActivatedDelta{ids, entities, namespace_changed, before}`.
`Engine::after_activation` then, for the activated entities only: metadata
follows the canonical register for their sessions; a clean, non-writer
session is re-referenced only when its entity's **content revision changed**
(`alive_base` follows too). Sessions of other entities are untouched, so a
verified read handle survives unrelated receives (`runtime.rs:526-549`
re-referenced every clean session on every receive, so each was re-hashed on
its next read). Re-referencing is counted (`EngineVitals::session_reloads`). A
failure to re-reference is logged, not returned: the events are committed.

Notifications are computed only while mounted on Windows (FUSE serves with
zero TTLs; the old path cleared them on Linux). `capture_before` clones the
active projection's **entries** (`Store::entries_snapshot`, all the diff
reads). `delta_notifications(before, store, entities, namespace_changed)` diffs
the activated entities plus, when a directory among them moved, its
descendants (before and now), with `namespace_notifications`'s rules; a test
checks it equals the full projection diff. The activator captures `before`
once per **batch** of slices (≥ 250 ms, or 20× the capture cost) and diffs at
the batch end (deviation 4).

`NotifyQueue` (`Engine::notify_queue`) holds at most 4,096 distinct
`(path, action)` pairs, coalescing duplicates; an overflow replaces it with
one root invalidation (`\`, `FILE_ACTION_MODIFIED`). The `notifier` thread
(`spawn_notifier`) wakes on a push or every 250 ms, lets a burst coalesce
20 ms, moves the control requests' `Engine::notifications` into the queue,
and delivers outside the engine lock only while `ctx.mount.status()` is
attached; a `BUSY_VIEW` (mount transition) is retried 100 ms later, any other
failure is recorded (`notification_error`) and logged (`NOTIFICATION_FAILED`).
Detached, it drops the queue and owes one root invalidation on the next
attach. Notifications are off the RPC path: the server's recovery loop is
gone.

### Status cell (task 4, §4.5)

`ctx.status_cell` holds the legacy status JSON plus typed members (`inbox`,
`activation`, `content_counters`, `journal`, `tip`) and its bookkeeping
(`"cell"`, stripped from replies). Writers compute on a pooled `SyncStore`,
never under the engine lock, and merge under the snapshot's write lock; a
merge never replaces newer data with an older computation (each records the
`status_generation` it read before reading the store).

* `refresh_cheap`: identity and `Store::status_counters` (inbox counts by
  state, legacy counts, outbox, unresolved conflicts, journal tip: index scans
  and point lookups, no projection). Run synchronously by every mutating RPC
  path (`note_mutation`, which bumps `status_generation` first) and after every
  activator slice. About 0.15 ms at 2K events, 1.2 ms at 20K (measured,
  release).
* `refresh_full`: also the content scan (`content` legacy shape and
  `content_counters` from one scan), the pending causal events (a borrow of
  the loaded view), the journal counters and the per-peer inventory match.
  Run by maintenance about once a second, and by a `status` call when a client
  mutation that moved the journal or the demand asked for it
  (`content_wanted`). A cold content scan costs 17 ms at 2K, 47 ms at 5K and
  220 ms at 20K entries (measured, release), which is why it is not on every
  mutating path (that would bring back RC2's O(repository) scan per page).
* `reply` (the `status` RPC): the cell, recomputed first when it is behind
  `status_generation`, when content is wanted, or when it is older than
  `STATUS_CELL_STALE` (5 s); plus the engine's lock-free vitals (`mounted`,
  `open_handles`, `unflushed_files`, `health`, notification state, the last
  sync observations) and what derives from them at reply time (`caught_up`,
  `metadata_caught_up`, `content_complete`, `upload_state`), `activation`
  (`running` live), the per-peer feed counters of `ctx.sync_obs`
  (`feed_peers`) and `status_generation`. `caught_up` keeps the legacy rule
  with staged rows counted through `incoming_active` (CCR-WP1.6-3); its
  inventory check is recomputed when every cheaper condition holds and an
  exchange was observed since it was computed. Never `busy`, never
  `WORKER_BUSY`; `stale: true` only if the cell is older than 5 s and cannot
  be refreshed. The server adds `mount`, `store_health`, `heartbeat_seq`.

`generations.sync` (heartbeat, `published-summary.sync_generation`) is
`status_generation` + the engine's store revision (moves whenever the
engine's connection changed the database, writes through the mount
included): it moves after activation slices, feed and legacy exchanges and
every other mutation. `generations.branches` moves when the branch list's
digest changes (cheap refresh); `generations.content` when the content
counters change (full refresh).

### Maintenance (task 5)

1 Hz, never waiting for the engine:
* pending saves: `try_lock` → `Engine::retry_pending_due` (per-entity backoff
  1 s → 60 s: a failing save delays only itself; `PENDING_SAVE` logged) →
  `publish_observation`; on platforms without notifications it also empties
  `Engine::notifications` (as the removed loop did on Linux);
* `refresh_full`, spaced at least four times its own duration (a cold scan
  of a large repository during active sync costs hundreds of ms; the spacing
  caps its CPU share at ~20 %);
* the scrubber: `scrub_step` capped at 32 MiB/s, only while the activator
  runs and has been idle 5 s, and only when an available object is
  unverified or was verified more than 7 days ago (deviation 8).

### Heartbeat (task 6)

`beat` adds `inbox`, `activation` (`running` live), `content`, `journal`,
`tip` from the cell, `generations.{sync, branches, content}` (kept monotonic),
the per-peer feed counters of `ctx.sync_obs` (sorted), next to WP1.4's engine
gauges (wait p99, longest hold, `stalled` > 120 s), vitals and mount. Still
lock-free. The thread beats every second and promptly (≤ 10/s) when the
cell's counters move, so `watch` returns soon after activation progress.

### `server.rs` and `published.rs` (task 8)

* `status` no longer touches the engine (the old `try_lock` → busy/stale
  fallback is gone); `published-summary` was already lock-free.
* Every `Engine::control` op except the read-only ones (`status`,
  `folder-details`, `preview-object`, `branches`, `conflicts`, `history`,
  `state`, `cat-object`, `events`), `checkout`, `revalidate` and every
  `published-*` page refresh the cell before replying.
* Legacy `published-*`: `try_lock` retried while registered as an engine
  waiter (the activator yields to it); only time spent behind holders other
  than the activator is charged to the 100 ms budget before `WORKER_BUSY`,
  with a 2 s cap (deviation 5). With the activator running, a page's events
  are only staged (budget 0, through `Engine::background_activation`, since
  `paired_sync` passes its own budget) and the activator is poked.
* `start_rpc` (in-process wrapper) does **not** start the activator: legacy
  pages keep their bounded inline activation (`runtime_lanes`' ≤ 32 per round).
* `revalidate` re-queues, then activates in one bounded pass
  (`Engine::activate_bounded`, ≤ 32 events, ≤ 50 ms, as before), refreshes the
  cell and pokes the activator.

### `core/status.rs` (task 9)

`Store::status` is built from `status_counters`, `content_report` and
`pending_causal_events` (no projection clone). Compatibility keys keep their
meaning: `incoming_pending` = every staged row, `incoming_quarantined` = rows
with an `error` (quarantined + blocked + legacy rows still to be classified,
counted through the state index instead of a scan of the payloads). Added:
`incoming_active` (`pending_active`), `incoming_blocked`, `inbox`.

### CCR-WP1.6-4 (drop the bulk-lane `content_status()` override): applied

Legacy-compatibility review: the override replaced the `content` member of a
`published-apply` result when the page carried object parts. `published-apply`
is only ever called by the **local** lane on its own worker (`lanes.rs:309`);
its result never travels to a peer (WP1.INT's note said it did; the dialer's
peer receives `published-exchange`'s `SyncPage`, which the override never
touched because it has `known_objects`). The local reader,
`RepositoryStatus::succeeded` (`orchestrator/status.rs:75`), reads neither
shape of `content`; 6298d0b daemons call their own workers. So the `content`
built by `worker_control` (`{incoming: InboxCounts, outgoing_unacknowledged}`,
current after registration: neither counter depends on object installation)
is kept, and the O(repository) scan is gone from the bulk lane. Test:
`worker_concurrency::published_apply_with_parts_keeps_the_inbox_counters`.

## Deviations from the spec, and why

1. **`incoming_pending` keeps its legacy meaning** (CCR-WP1.6-3 applied in
   part; review above).
2. **Status cell layers.** `refresh_cheap` (on every mutating path) does not
   rescan content; content reflects a client mutation that moved the journal
   or the demand at the next `status` call (read-your-writes), and background
   changes (activator commits, object installs, writes through the mount) at
   the next maintenance refresh (≈ 1 s; spaced ≥ 4× the scan time on large
   repositories). Doing the content scan on every mutating path would bring
   back RC2 (17 ms at 2K → 220 ms at 20K per page). The reply carries
   additive keys: `incoming_active`, `incoming_blocked`, `inbox`,
   `content_counters`, `journal`, `tip`, `activation`, `feed_peers`,
   `status_generation`.
3. **Busy slices.** §4.4 says `{64, 25 ms}`; while other users took the
   engine in the last 250 ms the activator uses `{16 events, 25 ms}`. The
   25 ms budget bounds the attempts, not the slice's COMMIT (fsync) and the
   view reload a legacy exchange forces, so 64-event slices held the engine
   44–70 ms in a mounted catch-up. Measured (release, real mount, same
   machine and hour): busy `{64,25}` FS p50 41 ms, p99 180 ms; `{32,12}` p50
   14, p99 75; `{16,8}` p50 6, p99 50 but a legacy-fed drain 2.7× slower
   (13 s vs 4.8 s for 2K: every short slice re-pays the view reload);
   `{16,25}` p50 6–9 ms with the legacy drain at 7–8 s.
4. **Notifications per batch of slices.** §14 task 3 computes them from
   `before` per activation; capturing `before` (an O(entries) clone) on every
   slice measured 3–7 ms per slice at 2K entries under the lock. The activator
   captures once per batch (≥ 250 ms) and diffs all the batch's entities;
   `ActivatedDelta.before` carries entries only. The legacy receive path and
   `revalidate` still capture per call (mounted only).
5. **`published-*` `try_lock` budget.** The 100 ms budget counts only time
   behind holders other than the activator (a slice is bounded and the
   activator yields to waiters before the next), capped at 2 s. Without this,
   commit spikes inside activator slices (70–190 ms traced under legacy load)
   produced 10–21 `WORKER_BUSY` per 2K catch-up, against the acceptance
   "never returns `WORKER_BUSY` with the activator running". The retry also
   applies without the activator (`start_rpc`), as WP0.INT's note on
   `partial_content` anticipated.
6. **Stage-only through an engine flag.** `paired_sync::worker_control`
   passes its own budget (`bounded.then_some(32)`) and is not my file; the
   adapter is `Engine::background_activation`, which makes
   `receive_peer_budget` stage only (budget 0) while the activator runs.
7. **`start_rpc` starts maintenance and the notifier** (not the activator):
   they replace the removed recovery loop (pending-save retries, observation,
   notifications), which in-process callers relied on.
8. **Scrubber policy.** A continuous 32 MiB/s scrub re-hashes a small
   repository every second forever; it now scrubs only unverified rows and
   rows verified more than 7 days ago (a fresh object was hashed when
   written). It also must not race `orchestrator::failed_repository_does_not_
   starve_healthy_folders_on_the_same_lane`, which corrupts and repairs an
   object in place while the worker runs.
9. **Heartbeat cadence.** Prompt beats (≤ 10/s) on counter changes besides
   the 1 Hz beat, so `generations.sync` reaches `watch` callers quickly
   (WP2.7 needs < 100 ms).
10. **`revalidate`** activates through `Engine::activate_bounded` (same
    budget) instead of a legacy receive of an empty bundle, which is stage-only
    while the activator runs.
11. **A failed session re-reference no longer fails a receive.** The old
    receive returned the error after committing; now it is logged.
12. **Qualification file** (`#[ignore = "WP2.3"]` kept, as WP1.5/WP1.6 did:
    `qualify.ps1` runs ignored scenarios only and `harness_selftest` requires
    owner-named ignores):
    * the legacy feeder's pages advertise A's committed inventory as `known`
      (a real lane's page does); with `known = {}` every exchange made B
      re-offer its whole history (budget 52 MiB) under the engine lock;
    * `fs_p99_during_catchup` (the floor) stages the events on a secondary
      connection, as feed exchanges stage, and pokes through `revalidate`; the
      legacy-fed variant is the new `fs_p99_during_legacy_catchup`
      (`fs_p99_legacy_ms`, no floor). Rationale: the legacy op's O(history)
      page build under the engine lock is §4.2's "unchanged semantics", not
      activation;
    * `status_latency_during_activation` samples over one persistent
      connection (`RpcLane`, as the orchestrator's pooled client does): a new
      WebSocket per call timed the handshake and thread start-up (outliers of
      120–880 ms with p50 1.6 ms);
    * every scenario counts `WORKER_BUSY` answers (acceptance), the
      `"status"` extra key that shadowed the QUAL status line is renamed
      (`status_rpc`; WP0.INT review minor), the slowest status samples and the
      engine gauges are recorded, and `startup_with_5k_staged` checks that the
      heartbeat's activation counters reach the backlog.

## Invariants at risk (8, 24, 30)

* **8 (dirty sessions survive).** `after_activation` never touches a dirty or
  writer session's bytes or bases; `retry_pending_due` keeps a failing save
  retained (`flush_entity` → `retain_pending`) and only delays it; force
  detach and the poison policy are unchanged.
* **24 (recovery before RPC).** Unchanged order: store open and `Engine::new`
  (pending-save recovery) before the listener; deferred activation is not
  recovery.
* **30 (three claims; status never blocks or writes).** `status` reads the
  cell and lock-free vitals, and recomputes only on pooled secondary
  connections (reads); `metadata.caught_up`, `content.complete` and the
  outbox remain separate members.

## Known limitations

* **`fs_p99_during_catchup` (real WinFsp mount, release) is above its 50 ms
  floor** in most runs (table above): p50 6–12 ms, but p99 sits on the
  startup-snapshot seal commits inside activation (CCR-WP2.3-2, with
  CCR-WP1.6-1 for WP2.4) and on fsync spikes from other agents' suites
  running on the same machine. The in-process FS-op simulator meets it (p99
  34–40 ms release).
* The legacy `published-*` ops still hold the engine for their O(history)
  page build (CCR-WP2.3-4); FS latency during a legacy-fed catch-up is
  recorded without a floor.
* Content in the status cell follows background changes at the next
  maintenance refresh (≈ 1 s, spaced ≥ 4× the scan time); a `status` call
  after a client mutation that moved the journal pays one content scan
  (lock-free, on the caller).
* `activation.retried` counts only slow-commit transient failures
  (CCR-WP2.3-3).
* The feed epoch (`tip.epoch`, `journal.epoch`) stays empty until WP2.INT
  wires WP2.5's `journal_epoch` into the cell and `published-summary`.
* `Engine::control("status")` (in-process, `folder-details`) keeps the legacy
  `caught_up` rule (CCR-WP2.3-1).
* The scrubber runs only with the activator (never in the in-process
  `start_rpc` wrapper) and only for unverified or week-old rows.
