## Reproduction and causes (with timestamps)

`tests/qual_wp5_3_recovery.rs` reproduces the lab's S3 scenario on one
machine: daemon A (source, listener) and daemon B (dialer, receiver), 4 lanes
(1 control + 3 bulk, as in the lab), B's lanes through a `tests/support`
`FaultProxy` capped at 8 MiB/s (the lab's tunnel ran at about 7 MB/s), a
512 MiB object, and a 45 s outage once a fifth of it is durable at B. Two
kinds of outage:

* **clean close** (the lab's `ssh -L` kill): `refuse(true)` + `reset_all()`:
  every connection is shut down at once and new ones are closed at accept;
* **silent blackhole** (a Wi-Fi drop): `blackhole(true)`: connections stay
  open, every byte is dropped, new connections' too.

Each run records, on the scenario's clock, every lane transition of B (and of
A under mutual pairing) with its backoff deadline (the `status` op, every
100 ms), B's durable spool offset (`spool_state`), and the wire counters.
Baseline: the wave 5 base (`c6948c9`), release build, this machine.

**Clean close, baseline** (`t` from the receive; outage 13.66 → 58.66 s):
every lane went to `Backoff` at 14.15 s with 0.5–0.8 s, and doubled its own
delay through the outage (the blackhole run below shows the sequence). After
the restore, lane 2 (the one that owns the object) was `Ready` at 77.59 s
(18.9 s after the restore), lanes 1, 0, 3 at 78.63, 79.47, 81.57 s (each on
its own timer). B's durable offset moved at 77.79 s, 0.2 s after lane 2
reconnected: the transfer itself resumed at once, from the durable offset
(1.004 wire bytes per byte left).

**Blackhole, baseline** (outage 13.32 → 58.32 s): the sessions died by
their liveness deadline 17–20 s into the outage (30.35–33.37 s). Each lane
then redialed into the silent link, each dial hanging about 3 s in its
handshake, then backing off 0.9, 2.0, 3–4.4, 8.5–9.3, 15–17.5 s, each lane
on its own schedule. One lane happened to start a dial 0.1 s after the
restore and was `Ready` at once; lane 0 had started one 0.07 s *before* the
restore, which hung into the restored link, failed and backed off 17.6 s:
the last lane was `Ready` 20.5 s after the restore.

**Mutual pairing, baseline**: lanes 20.9 s / 20.4 s after the restore (clean /
blackhole), bytes 12.3 s / 20.5 s (the first path whose lane came back,
B's pull or A's push).

The causes, in the order of the spec's list:

1. **Backoff cap.** Each lane doubles its own delay (1 s → 60 s) through the
   outage, so a lane redials 16–32 s apart by the time a 45 s outage ends:
   the restore is found up to 32 s late (the lab: 36 s; here 18.9 s for the
   first lane). This was the whole lanes-connected delay in every run.
2. **Sibling lanes.** Lanes reconnect on their own timers, seconds apart
   (clean: 4 s from the first to the last; blackhole: 20 s), and a dial
   begun into the dead link just before the restore hangs, fails and backs
   off for its full (long) delay although its siblings are up.
3. **Lease TTL.** Not a cause on this base. The lab's extra 50 s between
   "lanes connected" and "transfer resumed" (on `e8c2979`) was the lease of
   the lost session lapsing (60 s) plus the stall backoff the lapse imposes
   on the source (60 s); WP4.5's release of a failed round's leases removed
   it, and every baseline run here resumed 0.2 s after the owning lane
   reconnected. Two gaps remained, both closed (*Leases* below): leases a
   round kept for the next round when the session ended between two rounds,
   and an acceptor's offer whose session died between `objects.wants` and
   the push.
4. **Want scheduling.** Not a cause of the outage delay (a new lane runner's
   first round wants the object at once, at its durable offset). It was the
   cause of the demand latency: a round busy with a large object asks its
   worker for more objects only when its plan is empty, so a demanded object
   waited for the large object's plan or the 10 s round cap, and an idle
   lane did not react to new demand (the content generation bump is absorbed
   as an echo of the lane's own exchanges for 3 s after each one).

## What changed

### 1. Lane reconnect (`peer.rs`)

* **Network loss.** A lane that was healthy and loses its session by the
  transport (`CONNECTION_LOST`, `TRANSPORT_DEAD`, `WS_CLOSED`; not a
  `GOAWAY`, and not a close of this side's own: a lane runner that gave up
  or could not start, marked by `spawn_runner`) starts the peer's network
  loss. Until a lane connects again, every lane whose dial reaches nothing
  (`DialError::Connect`, `Timeout`) or whose session the transport loses
  redials on **one cadence shared by the peer's lanes**: the first redial one base delay (1 s, ±20 %) after the
  loss, then one per spacing, `age / 30` clamped to [base, cap] (1 s for the
  first 30 s, 1.5 s at 45 s, 4 s at 2 min, 60 s from 30 min on), ±20 %.
  Paced redials do not escalate the lane's own backoff. This is the "lower
  cap for transient network loss": a short outage is found within a spacing
  of its end, while a long one costs one dial per peer per spacing (30 dials
  in the first 30 s, about 150 in 30 min, then one a minute) instead of one
  per lane per doubling backoff.
* **Siblings.** When a lane connects, the peer's network loss ends, every
  sibling whose wait is *wakeable* dials at once, and every sibling dial
  that is stale (begun before the successful one, no `HELLO_ACK` yet,
  running 500 ms longer than the successful one took) is interrupted and
  redialed at once. A wait is wakeable when the lane reached no session
  (its dial failed) or the down peer paces it; a lane backing off after a
  session that ended otherwise (one the peer closed before it was healthy:
  a flapping acceptor) keeps its own escalating delay. A dial that fails
  while a sibling connects, or a paced lane whose sibling connected since
  its session ended, retries at once too. Mechanism: each attempt (dial or
  wait) registers a child of the supervisor's `Cancel` with what it is
  (`Supervisor::arm`, `Attempt::{Dial, Wait{wakeable}}`); `lane_connected`
  cancels the tokens of stale dials and wakeable waits; the dial is
  `dial_cancellable` on that token.
* **Healthy.** A lane is healthy once `Ready` for `LANE_HEALTHY_AFTER` (5 s,
  `PeerOptions::lane_reset_after`; §3.7 said 30 s): its backoff resets, and
  its loss by the transport is a network loss. A session the peer closes
  before that (a flapping acceptor) keeps escalating, as before. The reset
  is `run_lane`'s explicit computation only: the lane's `Backoff` gets no
  `success` call (its own healthy-period reset is disabled,
  `reset_after = Duration::MAX`), so a paced redial, which records no
  failure, can no longer carry a stale healthy period past an unhealthy
  session.
* Unchanged: a peer never reached since the supervisor started, a `GOAWAY`,
  a refusal, `Incompatible` / `Unauthorized` blocks (their gate) keep the
  per-lane backoff. Every redial is a fresh session (invariant 16).
* Logs: one line when a network loss begins and one when it ends
  (`{peer, lane, down_ms, redials}`), both under the code of the session
  loss that began it (no new code: `contracts` checks every code literal
  against the §8 table).

### 2. Leases of a dead session (`objects.rs`, `inbound_objects.rs`)

"Session loss notifies the worker" (the spec's first option), at every place
a lease of a lost session could survive:

* **Dialer, a round in flight** (unchanged since WP4.5): a failed round
  releases its unfinished objects.
* **Dialer, leases kept between rounds** (new): a round that reached its cap
  keeps its unfinished objects' leases for the next round. The lane now
  remembers them per repository (`LaneRun::kept`, entries dropped after a
  lease TTL without being planned again), and when the lane ends (session
  lost, or told to stop; not while the daemon stops, for adoption) it gives
  them up (`release_kept`), holding each key meanwhile (`scheduler::hold_key`,
  the process-wide one-exchange-per-key claim) so no round of the old runner
  or of the next session's overlaps the release. Before, they stayed hidden
  from every other source until they lapsed (60 s).
* **Acceptor, an offer without a push** (new): the acceptor has no word of a
  session's end (CCR-WP5.3-2), so it watches for it. Every `objects.*`
  request stamps its session (`seen_session`); a reaper thread (started with
  the first offer, every 500 ms) gives up the leases of an offer once the
  offer and its session have both been silent for `OFFER_GRACE` (5 s), with
  no backoff. The offer stays (marked): a dialer still there settles it at
  its next request exactly as before; a late push still writes (a free lease
  is taken by the write) unless another session took it (then
  `IN_FLIGHT_ELSEWHERE`). A dialer pushes right after its wants on the same
  peer slot, so only a dead session (or a dialer that pushes nothing and
  stays silent) is reaped. Push lease ids are per dialer lane
  (`push:<peer>:<i>/<n>`), so the lane's next session shares the dead
  one's: the reaper judges and marks each offer under the offers' lock
  right before its release and keeps its key marked as *reaping* until the
  release ends; `take_offer` (the next `objects.wants` or `objects.push` of
  that lane) waits for it (at most `RELEASE_DEADLINE` + 1 s), and an offer
  an exchange took or replaced first is not reaped (its leases are that
  exchange's). `wants` computes its worker deadline after that wait.
* **Bytes that arrived when a session ended** (`LaneRun::cancelled`): the
  supervisor cancels a lane's runner whenever its session ends, so a round
  that saw the cancel before the closed session dropped what had arrived
  instead of writing it (up to a window of bytes re-sent). Only a cancel
  while the session is still open (the lane told to stop) drops now.

Invariant 19: every release ends only leases held under the releasing
lane's own lease id (`give_up` checks it), and never overlaps an exchange
of that lane's next session (above); the worker stays the only arbiter (a
write of a lease another session holds is `IN_FLIGHT_ELSEWHERE`),
`Lane::owns` sharding is unchanged, and no lease is ever taken over. Invariant 16: no
request is replayed; recovered transfers are fresh pulls from the worker's
durable offset.

### 3. Demand (`objects.rs`, `scheduler.rs`, `objects_rpc.rs`, `demand.rs`)

* **Worker op `objects.demand{route, lane?, max_items?}`** →
  `{items:[{hash, requested_ms}]}`: the objects a reader waits for
  (`Store::demanded_objects`: `object_requests` younger than 24 h whose
  availability is not `available`, newest first, two index lookups per row),
  the lane's own with `lane`. Read-only: no lease, no backoff. Routed by the
  existing `objects.` prefix; its name and types live in
  `runtime::objects_rpc` until the model takes them (CCR-WP5.3-3).
* **Busy round** (`promote_demand`): while a download round has pulls in
  flight or a plan, every `DEMAND_POLL` (500 ms, counted from the end of the
  previous probe, so a slow answer never makes probes back to back) it
  probes `objects.demand`;
  demanded objects it planned move to the front of its plan (the next pull
  carries them); one it has not planned is leased with a small want
  (`DEMAND_WANT_ITEMS` = 16, the id a want with a pull in flight uses),
  whose `Demand` items go to the front. A worker that answers
  `UNSUPPORTED_OPERATION` (an adopted older build) is remembered per
  instance and not asked again.
* **Idle lane**: `Signals` gains `requested` (the heartbeat's
  `content.requested`); the download job is triggered when it grows,
  whatever the absorb window (a reader waiting is never an echo of ours).

## Changed assertions

* `peer_supervisor::the_default_policy_matches_section_3_7`:
  `lane_reset_after` 30 s → 5 s, and a new line for
  `network_spacing_ratio` = 30. The package section asks to "reset the
  backoff once the lane is healthy"; the 30 s of §3.7 was the "healthy"
  threshold (CCR-WP5.3-1 asks §3.7 and `limits::LANE_RESET_AFTER` to
  follow). No other existing assertion changed.

## Deviations

1. **A shared redial cadence rather than a lower per-lane cap.** A lower
   per-lane cap alone (say 2 s) does not bound a silent outage's recovery:
   a dial into a silent link hangs about 3 s (the session's handshake
   timeouts, not owned here), the lanes' dials start together when their
   sessions die together, and N lanes cost N dials per cap for as long as
   the peer is away. The cadence (one slot per spacing for the whole peer,
   taken by whichever lane is free) spreads dials over the lanes, bounds the
   restore → first fresh dial by about max(spacing, handshake hang / lanes),
   and costs less than the old policy over a long outage. The per-lane
   backoff (1 s → 60 s) still governs everything that is not a network loss.
2. **"Healthy" is 5 s Ready** (§3.7: 30 s). It decides both the backoff
   reset (the spec's item) and whether a loss starts the peer's network
   loss; a flapping acceptor still escalates.
3. **The acceptor's dead-session offers are released after 5 s of silence**,
   not at the session's end: the inbound server (`inbound.rs`, frozen) does
   not tell `inbound_objects` when a session closes (CCR-WP5.3-2). The lab's
   symptom did not involve it (the dialer's leases are released by the
   failed round at once).
4. **`objects.demand` is a new worker op defined outside the model**
   (`runtime::objects_rpc::{DEMAND, DemandHeader, DemandItem, DemandReply}`;
   CCR-WP5.3-3). It is routed by the existing `objects.` prefix; an older
   worker answers `UNSUPPORTED_OPERATION`, which the lane remembers.
5. **Idle-lane demand rides the heartbeat's `content.requested`**, which the
   status cell (WP5.4's file) refreshes on its own schedule; the busy-lane
   path (the common case: content is missing because it is still
   downloading) does not depend on it.

## Fix round 1 (review of `07ef4fd`)

Every finding was verified against the code; none is rejected.

1. **Blocker: the sibling wake defeated the per-lane backoff of a flapping
   peer** (`peer.rs`). Confirmed: `lane_connected` cancelled every waiting
   sibling, and every wait was registered as wakeable, so lanes whose
   sessions the peer closed right away woke each other at about one session
   per dial time. Reproduced by the new
   `a_flapping_peer_keeps_escalating_over_a_slow_link` (4 lanes, 200 ms →
   5 s, every chunk held 30 ms each way): 94 and 95 sessions in 9 s at a
   constant rate on `07ef4fd`, 24 (16 in the first 3 s, 4 from 6 to 9 s)
   with the fix. **Fix**: a wait records whether a connect may cut it short
   (`Attempt::Wait{wakeable}`, `wakeable = dial_failed || paced`): only a
   lane that reached no session or that the down peer paces is woken; a
   lane backing off after a session that ended otherwise keeps its
   escalating delay. Why it cannot loop: a connect wakes only lanes whose
   last attempt reached no session or that are paced; every lane that
   connects and loses its session unhealthy then waits out its own
   escalating delay (unwakeable), so each lane's connects are bounded by its
   own backoff or by healthy (≥ 5 s) sessions, and pacing exists only after
   a healthy session's loss and ends at the first connect. The "a sibling
   connected meanwhile: try now" shortcut applies only to wakeable waits;
   for a lost session its reference count of connects is re-read when the
   session ends (not at its dial, which would count the lane's own
   connect). The module docs, the lane table and the sibling test's doc say
   so.
2. **Minor: a paced redial kept `Backoff`'s stale healthy period.**
   Confirmed (`success` sets `healthy_since` only once; only `failure` or
   `reset` clears it, and a paced redial calls neither). **Fix**: the lane
   no longer calls `success`, and its `Backoff` is built with
   `reset_after = Duration::MAX`: the attempt count resets only through
   `run_lane`'s explicit "Ready for `lane_reset_after`". The existing
   `backoff_resets_after_a_healthy_session` and
   `reconnect_backoff_stays_within_jitter_bounds` still pass unchanged.
3. **Minor: a local close counted as a transport loss.** Confirmed

4. **Minor: demand probes could run back to back.** Confirmed. **Fix**:

## Known limitations

* **A long outage reconnects later**: the spacing grows with the outage's
  age (4 s at 2 min, 20 s at 10 min, 60 s from 30 min on), so the restore is
  found within that. A peer never reached since its supervisor started (the
  daemon started during the outage) keeps the per-lane backoff (1 s → 60 s).
* **A silent link is detected by the session's liveness deadline (20 s)**;
  until then the lanes look `Ready`. Dials into it hang about 3 s each (the
  session's handshake timeouts): with N lanes the restore is found within
  about max(spacing, 3 s / N) plus a dial.
* **The acceptor's offer of a dead session** is released after 5 s of
  silence (CCR-WP5.3-2), not at once. An exchange of that lane's next
  session that arrives during the release waits for it (one
  `objects.release`, at most 3 s); past that bound it goes on, and the
  release may end a lease it was offered (a later write re-grants a free
  lease; the worker still arbitrates).
* **A local close racing a remote one**: a session the peer closes while
  its lane runner is giving up on it counts as closed here (per-lane
  backoff, from the base delay after a healthy session), not as a network
  loss.
* **A flapping peer** (sessions closed before they are healthy) is redialed
  on each lane's own escalating backoff (up to 60 s per lane). A sibling
  whose dials fail meanwhile (it reached no session) is still woken by each
  connect of those lanes, so it redials at most once per such connect.
* **A demanded object inside its failure backoff** (a source that failed it,
  a local error) waits for the backoff; demand reorders, it does not retry.
  An adopted worker of an older build (no `objects.demand`) gets demand
  first only at the start of each round, as before.
* **Idle-lane demand** depends on the status cell refreshing
  `content.requested` (deviation 5).
* The scenarios run on one machine through `FaultProxy`; the two-machine lab
  is the coordinator's to re-run.
